Week 1: My first meeting with Mary, my business analyst from the BMAD Method

I'm a marathon runner, and I'm building my own running app. I have no development team and a budget of $0.

What I do have is the BMAD Method: an open-source framework that turns AI into a full project team. Each AI agent has a job title, such as business analyst, product manager, architect or developer. They do the work. I'm the Vibe CEO: I make the decisions and check the results.

This week I had my first meeting, with Mary, my business analyst. She ran a brainstorming session using four techniques: Role Playing, Five Whys, Cross-Pollination and Six Thinking Hats. They're just 4 of the 108 brainstorming techniques built into the BMAD Method.

Five Whys surprised me most. I arrived with the question every marathoner asks: can I hit my goal time?

Mary asked why. Then she asked again, five times in all.
Why 1: so I can adjust my training before it's too late.
Why 2: because fitness takes time to build. About 3 weeks before a marathon, the "hay is in the barn."
By Why 5, we'd reached the real problem: runners have no honest early-warning voice. Our apps cheer us on. None of them warn us.

I know that problem first-hand. In the middle of an intense training cycle, I woke up at night with glute pain. I'd ramped up too much, too fast, and my app never said a word.

Mary summed up what we'll build in one line: "Most running apps cheer you on. This one gets you to the finish line, injury-free."

By the end of the session we had 77 ideas: 50 mine, 27 Mary's. My favourite of hers is "Taper Jail," a cheeky warning for runners who sneak in extra miles during race week.

Then I made the calls:
- What goes into the first version
- What waits for a later update (calorie tracking)
- What we won't build (an iPhone app, user accounts, payments)

Every decision is written down, so anyone can see how we got here.

Next week: Mary and I will do technical research and work on the product brief.

Curious about the details? Every file BMAD has produced so far is in the GitHub repository: https://github.com/anitakwanthetechie/Runner-App-and-the-BMAD-Method
